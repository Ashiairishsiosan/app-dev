# Barry Lyndon: The Rise and Fall of an Irish Rogue

> *A young Irishman claws his way up the social ladder of 18th-century Europe, only to learn that fortune gives and takes away with equal indifference.*

---

## A. Introduction

### Overview

**Barry Lyndon** is a 1975 period drama written, produced, and directed by **Stanley Kubrick**. It is based on the 1844 novel *The Luck of Barry Lyndon* by William Makepeace Thackeray. The film follows the life of Redmond Barry, an ambitious and flawed young man who travels across Europe in search of wealth and status. Over a running time of about three hours, it paints a slow, deliberate, and painterly portrait of 18th-century aristocratic life. Although it was not a major box office hit on release, it is now widely regarded as one of the finest films ever made.

### Quick Facts

- **Genre:** Historical period drama (with elements of romance and war)
- **Release year:** 1975
- **Director:** Stanley Kubrick
- **Runtime:** About 185 minutes
- **Country:** United Kingdom and United States

---

## B. Synopsis

The story begins in rural Ireland, where young **Redmond Barry** falls for his cousin Nora Brady. When she chooses to marry a wealthy British officer, Captain Quin, Barry challenges him to a duel and believes he has killed him. He flees from home, is robbed on the road, and ends up joining the British army during the Seven Years' War. After deserting, he is forced into the Prussian army, where he later becomes a police spy and is assigned to watch a gambler known as the Chevalier de Balibari. Barry joins forces with the Chevalier instead, and the two travel from court to court across Europe, cheating wealthy nobles at cards.

Seeking a more comfortable life, Barry sets his sights on the rich and lonely **Lady Lyndon**. After her elderly husband dies, he marries her, takes her family name, and becomes **Barry Lyndon**. His new wealth does not bring him respect, because he spends recklessly, mistreats his wife, and fails to win the acceptance of high society. His stepson, **Lord Bullingdon**, hates him and grows more hostile over the years. Barry's only real joy is his young son Bryan, and when Bryan dies in a riding accident, Barry is shattered. In the end, Bullingdon forces a final duel, Barry is badly wounded and loses his leg, and he is stripped of his fortune and sent back to Ireland a broken man.

### Story at a Glance

1. Barry fights a duel over Nora Brady and flees Ireland.
2. He serves in two armies and works as a spy.
3. He becomes a professional gambler alongside the Chevalier.
4. He marries Lady Lyndon and takes her name.
5. His son dies and his stepson takes revenge.
6. Barry loses everything and returns to Ireland.

## C. Characters

- **Redmond Barry / Barry Lyndon** (Ryan O'Neal): A charming, hot-tempered Irish rogue who dreams of rising above his station. His ambition carries him far, but his pride and cruelty cause his downfall.
- **Lady Lyndon** (Marisa Berenson): A wealthy, quiet, and emotionally fragile noblewoman. She is married to Barry for his advantage and suffers greatly under his neglect.
- **Lord Bullingdon** (Leon Vitali): Lady Lyndon's son from her first marriage. He despises Barry from childhood and eventually becomes the man who brings him down.
- **The Chevalier de Balibari** (Patrick Magee): A veteran gambler and fellow Irishman who mentors Barry in the art of cheating at cards across the courts of Europe.
- **Captain Potzdorf** (Hardy Kruger): A Prussian officer who recruits Barry as a spy and later becomes a reluctant link in his story.
- **Nora Brady** (Gay Hamilton): Barry's first love, whose choice of a wealthy suitor sets the entire tragedy in motion.
- **The Narrator** (Michael Hordern): A calm, ironic voice that tells us what will happen before it happens, giving the film its sense of fate.

## D. Personal Review

### Why I Chose This Film

I chose *Barry Lyndon* because it is often described as a masterpiece of visual storytelling, and I wanted to see whether a three-hour period drama could hold my attention. I was also curious about Stanley Kubrick's work outside his better-known genres such as horror and science fiction.

### What I Liked

1. **The cinematography.** Many scenes look like moving oil paintings. The film famously used special ultra-fast lenses to shoot interior scenes lit only by candlelight, and the result is soft, warm, and unlike anything else.
2. **The ironic storytelling.** The narrator reveals future events in advance, so the tension comes not from what happens but from watching how fate catches up with Barry.
3. **The costumes and sets.** Every costume and location feels authentic to the 18th century, which makes the world completely believable.

### My Rating

**9 / 10**

### Recommendation

I recommend *Barry Lyndon* to anyone who enjoys slow, thoughtful cinema, beautiful visuals, and stories about ambition and its consequences. It is *not* a fast-paced film, so viewers who prefer action or quick plots may find it difficult at first. If you are patient and watch it with full attention, it is one of the most rewarding films you can see.
